# Independent Love-Wave Forward Validation

## Purpose

The main inversion experiment uses an analytical three-layer Love-wave model both to generate and to invert the data. This is appropriate for isolating mode-level information, but it can produce an inverse-crime bias. The purpose of this validation is not to estimate field accuracy. It is to test two narrower questions:

1. Are the analytical roots reproduced by an independent layered-media formulation?
2. Do the predicted fundamental and higher Love branches produce coherent dispersion peaks in a wavefield generated by time-domain wave propagation?

## Validation A: Transfer-matrix root check

The analytical arctangent secular equation used in the paper was compared with an independently implemented Haskell-Thomson state-transfer matrix.

- Frequency points: 24 from 100 to 560 Hz.
- Seam models: 1.8, 2.8 and 3.8 m.
- Total model-frequency combinations: 72.
- Exact modal-count agreement: 70/72 (97.2%).
- Non-empty modal-set agreement: 68/70 (97.1%).
- Where modal sets match, phase-velocity roots agree to numerical precision.
- The two count mismatches occur close to a high-velocity cutoff boundary.

This check shows that the modal branches in the main paper are not an artifact of the particular arctangent implementation.

## Validation B: 2D SH finite-difference wavefield

### Acquisition and model

- 2D heterogeneous SH displacement finite difference.
- Grid spacing: 0.5 m.
- Model length: 300 m.
- Three seam thicknesses: 1.8, 2.8 and 3.8 m.
- Coal shear velocity: 1120 m/s.
- Roof and floor shear velocities: 2400 and 2650 m/s.
- Receivers: 200, spaced at 1 m, starting 20 m from the source.
- Record length: 0.32 s.
- Sources: Ricker wavelets centered at 240 and 400 Hz.

Dispersion was extracted using a normalized phase-shift coherence transform. A predicted mode was counted as observed when a local coherence maximum fell within a frequency-dependent aperture-limited tolerance and passed coherence and contrast thresholds. This is a guided validation against analytical curves, not a blind picking benchmark.

The transform retains the observed complex receiver spectrum and applies the
steering factor `exp(-i 2 pi f x / c)`, consistent with NumPy's
positive-frequency FFT convention for a wave propagating in the positive
`x` direction. An earlier implementation that used only receiver amplitudes
without the observed phase was removed before producing these results.

The receiver spacing was reduced from 2 to 1 m while preserving the 200-m
aperture. At 500 Hz, 2-m sampling cannot represent phase velocities below
approximately 2 km/s without spatial aliasing, which affected the slow
fundamental branch across much of the tested band.

### Results

| Mode | Predicted frequency points | Strictly matched | Detection | Median matched velocity error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fundamental | 70 | 7 | 10.0% | 17.9 m/s |
| First higher | 34 | 3 | 8.8% | 23.7 m/s |
| Second higher | 11 | 0 | 0.0% | N/A |

Detection improves in the higher-frequency band:

| Band | Fundamental | First higher | Second higher |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 100-240 Hz | 0/22 | 0/1 | 0/0 |
| 260-400 Hz | 0/24 | 0/13 | 0/1 |
| 420-560 Hz | 7/24 | 3/20 | 0/10 |

The finite-difference fields therefore contain coherent fundamental and higher Love branches, but not every theoretical mode is equally observable. Weak low-frequency modes are affected by source spectrum, leakage into the roof and floor, and finite receiver aperture. The strict detection percentages should not be interpreted as probabilities that a mode physically exists.

These percentages are also not directly comparable with the frequency-wise 93.6%, 60.9% and 31.3% availability rates in the main synthetic experiment. The main experiment samples mode presence in a synthetic image, whereas the finite-difference test additionally includes source spectrum, geometric spreading, leakage and finite-aperture beamforming.

## Interpretation

### What is validated

- The analytical Love roots agree with an independent matrix formulation.
- The theoretical branches generate coherent energy in an independent time-domain wavefield.
- Higher modes are observable in selected frequency bands.
- The claim that mode availability differs from theoretical mode existence is supported.

### What is not validated

- Field-data performance.
- Three-dimensional source radiation.
- Roadway waves and free-surface effects.
- HTI or orthorhombic anisotropy.
- Frequency-dependent Q.
- Attenuation extraction or joint inversion.
- A calibrated parameter posterior.

The validation is deliberately separate from the main inversion. The next technical task is to extend the SH model to a viscoelastic medium and feed the numerically extracted dispersion and attenuation into the full tracking and inversion workflow.

## Broadband SLS attenuation check

The fixed branch ordering exposed an additional problem in the earlier
paired-Q test: at frequencies where more than one root existed, the descending
velocity sort selected a near-cutoff higher branch rather than the fundamental.
The analytical root order is now fixed by physical mode number.

A five-mechanism standard linear solid was fitted over 80-550 Hz with mean
relative Q errors of about 4.0% for Q=40 and Q=300. The discrete constitutive
update uses auxiliary memory variables and a Crank-Nicolson scheme; a harmonic
test reproduces Q=38.50 for the Q=40 fit and Q=288.32 for the Q=300 fit at
280 Hz.

At the 280-Hz source frequency, modal-energy theory predicts an effective Q of
40.92, while the paired SLS wavefield gives 37.35. The relative difference is
8.7%. This is a forward consistency check for the first-order attenuation
approximation, not a frequency-dependent Q inversion or a field calibration.
